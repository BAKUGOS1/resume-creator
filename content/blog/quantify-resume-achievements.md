## Quick answer

Quantify an achievement using a number you can support: volume, time saved, cost, quality or frequency. State what you did and the result, with context such as the period measured. If you lack a reliable number, describe the outcome clearly instead of inventing a metric.

“Improved customer satisfaction” is a claim. “Raised CSAT from 82% to 91% in two quarters” is evidence. Numbers make bullets believable, easier to scan and easier to talk about in an interview.

You don’t need a sales quota or a data job to have numbers. Almost every role can be measured in one of five ways.

## 1. Scale: how much, how many, how big

Count the things you handled.

- People: team size, customers, users, students, patients
- Volume: tickets, orders, invoices, deployments, applications reviewed
- Scope: locations, products, budgets, codebases, accounts

> Before: Managed store accounting.
> After: Managed cashiering and bank reconciliation for 6 high-volume stores across 3 companies.

## 2. Speed: how much faster

Time saved is one of the easiest results to measure.

- Turnaround time, response time, build time, page load, cycle time
- Time to hire, time to close the books, time to resolve a ticket

> Before: Made month-end close faster.
> After: Cut month-end close from 8 working days to 5 by automating three reconciliations.

## 3. Money: earned, saved or protected

- Revenue generated or influenced
- Costs reduced, waste cut, spend avoided
- Losses prevented (fraud caught, errors fixed before payment)

> Before: Reduced cloud costs.
> After: Reduced AWS spend by $14k a month by right-sizing instances and adding autoscaling.

## 4. Quality: fewer errors, better outcomes

- Error rates, defects, incidents, returns, complaints
- Accuracy, satisfaction scores, test coverage, uptime

> Before: Improved software quality.
> After: Added a release gate (lint, unit and end-to-end tests), cutting production incidents by 60% year over year.

## 5. Frequency and consistency

How often you did something, or how reliably.

- “Published 40 articles a quarter”
- “Ran weekly stand-ups for 3 teams”
- “Maintained 99.95% uptime over 18 months”

## When you don’t have exact numbers

Be honest and approximate sensibly:

- **Use ranges or rounding:** “~200 tickets a month”, “10+ clients”.
- **Use before/after comparisons:** “from 3 days to same-day”.
- **Use relative terms backed by facts:** “the team’s highest-rated onboarding course (4.8/5)”.
- **Estimate with a method you could explain** in an interview. If you can’t defend the number, don’t use it.

Never invent figures. Interviewers ask “how did you measure that?” — have the answer.

## A formula that works

**Verb + what you did + measurable result + how (optional)**

- “Rebuilt the onboarding flow, lifting week-one activation from 31% to 38% by cutting two form steps.”
- “Trained 25 new staff on ERP and POS systems, reducing billing errors in their first month by half.”

Not every bullet needs a number. Aim for numbers in roughly half your bullets; Resume Creator’s health check suggests adding more when fewer than about a third include one.

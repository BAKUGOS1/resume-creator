## Quick answer

For most jobs, send a résumé or industry-style CV focused on relevant skills and experience. Use biodata only when the organisation explicitly requests it; it can include personal details that are unnecessary for a normal job application. Share sensitive information only when required and appropriate.

In India you'll hear three words: **biodata**, **CV** and **resume**. Using the wrong one won't usually cost you a job, but sending the right format does make a better first impression.

## Quick comparison

|                                               | Biodata                            | CV                               | Resume                                |
| --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Focus                                         | Personal details                   | Career and qualifications        | Fit for one specific job              |
| Typical length                                | 1–2 pages                          | 2+ pages                         | 1–2 pages                             |
| Personal info (DOB, religion, marital status) | Yes                                | Rarely                           | No                                    |
| Where it's used                               | Some government forms, matrimonial | Academic, research, senior roles | Private-sector and international jobs |

## Biodata

A biodata (short for _biographical data_) lists personal facts: date of birth, gender, parents' names, address, marital status, sometimes religion and a photo, followed by education and work history. It's still requested by some government and public-sector offices and is the norm for matrimonial profiles.

**Don't send a biodata for private-sector or multinational jobs.** Personal details like religion or marital status aren't relevant to the role and can invite bias.

## CV

In Indian hiring, "CV" usually means the same thing as a resume. Some employers, especially universities, research institutes and hospitals, use it in the academic sense: a complete record of degrees, publications, conferences and teaching. See [CV vs resume](/blog/cv-vs-resume/) for how this differs around the world.

## Resume

A resume is the concise, job-focused document most companies in India expect today: contact details, a short summary, experience with measurable results, education and skills. It's what applicant tracking systems at IT, consulting, banking and start-up employers are built to read.

## Which one should you send?

- **IT, private, start-up or MNC job:** resume (even if the post says "CV").
- **Campus placement:** a one-page resume; see the [fresher guide](/blog/fresher-resume-guide/).
- **University or research post:** a full academic CV.
- **Government form that asks for biodata:** follow its exact format.
- **Applying abroad:** a resume without personal details, in that country's spelling.

## Turning a biodata into a resume

1. Remove date of birth, religion, caste, marital status, parents' names and the photo.
2. Keep name, phone, email, city and LinkedIn.
3. Add a 2–3 line summary and rewrite duties as results.
4. Use a clean single-column layout ([ATS-friendly format](/blog/ats-resume-format/)).

“Responsible for”, “worked on” and “helped with” describe a job description, not what you achieved. Starting each bullet with a precise verb forces you to say what _you_ did — and recruiters scanning in seconds see the action first.

Pick the verb that matches the real work. Don’t reach for “spearheaded” if you joined halfway through.

## How to use a verb list

1. Write the bullet plainly first: “I made the onboarding emails better.”
2. Ask what you actually did: rewrote them? tested variants? automated them?
3. Pick the verb that says that: “Rewrote”, “A/B-tested”, “Automated”.
4. Add the result: “…lifting week-one activation from 31% to 38%.”

> Before: Responsible for a team of five engineers.
> After: Led a team of five engineers; mentored two to promotion within 18 months.

## Verbs to retire

| Instead of      | Try                                                          |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Responsible for | Led, Owned, Ran, Managed                                     |
| Worked on       | Built, Designed, Improved, Shipped                           |
| Helped          | Supported, Enabled, Contributed to (with your specific part) |
| Assisted with   | Drafted, Prepared, Coordinated                               |
| Was involved in | Name your actual role in it                                  |
| Utilised        | Used                                                         |

## A quick test for every bullet

Read the bullet without the verb. If it still makes sense, the verb is doing no work. Then check: does the bullet say what changed? If not, add the result — our guide to [quantifying achievements](/blog/quantify-resume-achievements/) shows how.

A first resume is hard because the usual advice assumes years of work history. As a fresher, recruiters judge you on **potential and evidence**: what you built, what you learned and how clearly you present it. Here’s how to make one page do that.

## The right section order for freshers

1. **Name and contact details** — phone, professional email, city, LinkedIn, GitHub or portfolio
2. **Headline** — the role you’re applying for, e.g. “Frontend Developer” or “Graduate Analyst”
3. **Summary** — two sentences
4. **Education** — degree, college, years, and your grade if it helps
5. **Projects** — your strongest evidence
6. **Internships / Experience** — including part-time work and freelancing
7. **Skills** — grouped
8. **Certifications, achievements, activities**

Education and projects come before experience because they are your strongest material. Once you have a year or two of work, move Experience up.

## Projects: your most important section

Pick 2–4 projects that match the job. For each, include:

- **Name and one-line description** — what it is and who it’s for
- **Tech stack or tools**
- **A link** — live site, GitHub or demo
- **2–3 bullets** on what _you_ did and what it achieved

> Weak: “Made an e-commerce website using MERN stack.”
> Strong: “Built a MERN e-commerce site with cart, payments (Razorpay test mode) and an admin dashboard; deployed on Vercel with 95+ Lighthouse performance.”

College projects, hackathons, open-source contributions and freelance work for local businesses all count.

## Internships and part-time work

Treat internships like jobs: title, company, dates, and results. Non-technical work counts too — retail, tutoring, operations or accounts work shows responsibility and reliability. Describe it with numbers: “Handled billing and cash reconciliation for a store with ~300 daily transactions.”

## Skills: group them, don’t rate them

```
Languages: JavaScript, TypeScript, Python, SQL
Frontend: React, Next.js, Tailwind CSS
Backend: Node.js, Express, PostgreSQL
Tools: Git, GitHub Actions, Figma, Postman
```

Skip progress bars and star ratings — they don’t parse and recruiters ignore them. Only list skills you could answer interview questions about.

## Common fresher mistakes

- **A long objective** about what you want. Replace it with a two-sentence summary of what you offer.
- **Personal details** like date of birth, father’s name or marital status. They’re not needed for most private-sector jobs and take space.
- **A photo** — unless the employer asks for one.
- **Listing every course** you took. Pick the ones relevant to the role.
- **Two pages.** One page is right for freshers.

## Quick answer

Use one page when it covers your relevant qualifications clearly. Use two pages when substantial relevant experience needs the space. Relevance and readable text matter more than a fixed page count; do not shrink the font excessively or keep unrelated roles just to fill space.

Resume length isn’t a rule, it’s a trade-off: enough detail to prove you can do the job, short enough to read in the few seconds a first screen takes. [Ladders’ eye-tracking study](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ladders-updates-popular-recruiter-eye-tracking-study-with-new-key-insights-on-how-job-seekers-can-improve-their-resumes-300744217.html) measured an initial scan of about 7.4 seconds and recommended a two-page maximum for experienced professionals.

## A simple guideline

| Experience                                | Length                                   |
| ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Student, fresher, 0–3 years               | 1 page                                   |
| 3–10 years                                | 1 page, or 2 if the extra page is strong |
| 10+ years, senior or leadership           | 2 pages                                  |
| Academic, research, some government roles | Longer CV formats are normal             |

The second page has to earn its place. If it only holds an old job and a skills list, cut it back to one.

## What to cut first

1. **Jobs older than 10–15 years** — keep the title, company and dates, drop the bullets.
2. **Bullets that describe duties** instead of results.
3. **Repeated skills** that already appear in your bullets.
4. **“References available on request”** — it’s assumed.
5. **Personal details** not required in your country.
6. **Extra white space from oversized margins** — 0.5–0.75 in is fine.

## If you’re slightly over one page

Try these before cutting good content:

- Tighten bullets to one or two lines each.
- Reduce spacing between sections a little.
- Use a denser template such as **Compact**.
- Lower the body text slightly — but not below about 10 pt.

Resume Creator’s **Fit to one page** option does the last step automatically: it shrinks text to fit, and warns you instead of going below a readable size.

## Making two pages work

- Put your strongest, most recent experience on page one.
- Repeat your name on page two (a small footer with your name and page number works).
- Don’t leave page two a quarter full; either fill it with valuable content or trim to one page.
- Keep sections from splitting awkwardly — a job title shouldn’t sit alone at the bottom of page one.

## What a strong one-page resume contains

For someone with 2–6 years of experience, a well-used single page typically fits:

- A headline and a 2–3 line summary
- 2–3 recent roles with 3–5 bullets each, older roles condensed
- 1–2 relevant projects (more for engineers, designers and students)
- A grouped skills section of 3–5 lines
- Education in 1–2 lines

If yours has all of that and still spills over, the bullets are probably too long. Aim for one to two lines each, starting with a [strong verb](/blog/resume-action-verbs/) and ending with a [measurable result](/blog/quantify-resume-achievements/).

## When a longer CV is expected

Academic and research positions, medical roles and some government applications ask for a full CV listing publications, grants, presentations and teaching. Length isn’t a weakness there — completeness is expected. Follow the posting’s instructions; if it asks for a “resume”, keep it short, and if it asks for a “CV” in academia, include everything relevant.

## Regional differences

- **US and Canada:** “resume”, usually 1–2 pages, no photo or personal details.
- **UK, Ireland, Australia, New Zealand:** often called a “CV”, commonly 2 pages.
- **India:** both terms are used. For private-sector roles, 1–2 pages without personal details like date of birth or marital status is now the norm.
- **Parts of Europe:** a photo may be customary — check local norms for the country you are applying to.
